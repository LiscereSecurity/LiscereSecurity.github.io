Services / Incident response for OT

Know what to do in the first hour of an OT incident.

  • What it is

    A response plan written for an industrial site: roles and decisions, how to isolate systems without losing control of the process, how to recover controllers and operator stations, and how to report on time. Tested with the team in a tabletop exercise.

  • Who it is for

    Industrial and critical infrastructure operators, especially entities covered by NIS2, and any site where an IT incident plan exists but says nothing about the plant floor.

  • Why it is needed

    Most incident plans are written for IT. In a plant, shutting a system down can be more dangerous than the attack itself. The decisions must be clear before an incident, not during it.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

How is it different from an IT plan?

In IT the usual response is to isolate and restore. In OT, cutting a connection can stop a line, leave a process in an unsafe state or blind the operators. An OT plan decides in advance what can be isolated, who decides to stop production and how safety is kept.

What does the plan include?

Roles and contacts, escalation and decision points, isolation and recovery procedures for controllers and operator stations, communication with vendors and authorities, and templates for the reports NIS2 requires.

What does NIS2 require for incident reporting?

An early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of a significant incident, a notification within 72 hours and a final report within one month, sent to the competent CSIRT or authority.

How is the plan tested?

A tabletop exercise walks the team through a realistic scenario, such as ransomware reaching the operator stations, and shows where the plan needs work.

Can it build on an existing IT plan?

Yes. Where an IT plan already exists, the OT plan connects to it, so both teams know who leads, who decides and how they work together during an incident.

How long does it take?

Typically a few weeks, depending on the number of sites and the documentation that already exists. Scope and price are agreed in a short first call and fixed up front.
