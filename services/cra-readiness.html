Services / CRA readiness

The CRA applies to the product, not the plant. Find out if that means you.

  • What it is

    A readiness assessment against Regulation (EU) 2024/2847: whether your products fall in scope, which Annex III category they belong to, the gap against the essential requirements of Annex I Part I and the vulnerability handling duties of Part II, and what the Article 14 reporting obligations mean in practice.

  • Who it is for

    Manufacturers of products with digital elements used in industry: machine builders, panel and gateway makers, device and software vendors, and industrial companies that place equipment on the market under their own name or trademark.

  • Why it is needed

    The reporting duties have applied since 11 September 2026 and the main obligations from 11 December 2027. The Joint Research Centre and ENISA found that no single standard covers all the requirements of the act, and every IEC 62443 part in their mapping is scoped to industrial systems, so deep OT competence does not translate automatically into conformity.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Does the CRA apply to us?

It binds the manufacturer of a product with digital elements, meaning whoever develops or manufactures it, or has it made, and places it on the EU market under their own name or trademark. A plant that only buys and runs equipment is not a manufacturer. But a company that badges a control panel, a gateway or an application as its own becomes the manufacturer of that product, and that is the case most industrial companies miss.

What are the dates?

The regulation entered into force on 10 December 2024. The chapter on notification of conformity assessment bodies applies from 11 June 2026, the reporting obligations of Article 14 from 11 September 2026, and the main obligations from 11 December 2027. Earlier EU type-examination certificates on cybersecurity requirements stay valid until 11 June 2028 unless they expire sooner.

Is IEC 62443 enough for CRA conformity?

No, and that is documented rather than assumed. The joint Joint Research Centre and ENISA analysis (EUR 31892 EN) states that there is not a single standard capable of covering all the requirements expressed in the act. It maps parts 3-2, 4-1, 4-2 and 2-1 against Annex I, with 4-2 matched against eight product requirements, but qualifies every 62443 entry with a scope note limiting it to industrial automation and control systems. The act is horizontal, so a standard scoped to industrial systems carries part of the argument and leaves a gap outside it.

What are the reporting duties?

For an actively exploited vulnerability: an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware, a full notification within 72 hours, and a final report no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available. For a severe incident affecting the security of the product: 24 hours, 72 hours, and a final report within one month of the 72 hour submission. Reports go to the CSIRT of the member state of your main establishment and to ENISA at the same time, through the CRA Single Reporting Platform.

What does the readiness assessment deliver?

A scoping decision on which of your products are in scope and under which Annex III category, a gap analysis against Annex I Part I and Part II, a review of the vulnerability handling process you would need over the support period, a view of the conformity assessment route open to you, and a plan ordered by what blocks market access first. Where the CENELEC work under M/606 bears on your product, we say which parts and what is still provisional.

How long does it take?

Typically a few weeks, depending on how many products are in scope and how much technical documentation already exists. Scope and price are agreed in a short first call and fixed up front, and shaped around the product range rather than sold as a fixed package.
