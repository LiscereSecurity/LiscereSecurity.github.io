Services / NIS2 readiness

NIS2 is law across the EU. The clock is already running.

  • What it is

    A readiness assessment for NIS2 and the national law that applies: which obligations apply, what is already in place, what is missing and the evidence auditors will ask for, with the OT side fully covered.

  • Who it is for

    Medium and large companies in the eighteen sectors NIS2 covers, such as energy, water, transport, chemicals, food and manufacturing, and the suppliers that serve them.

  • Why it is needed

    NIS2 brings strict duties on risk management, incident reporting and management accountability, with fines of up to 10 million euros or 2% of worldwide turnover. Most compliance projects leave the OT side out.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Is my company covered by NIS2?

Medium and large organisations in the sectors listed in Annexes I and II of the directive are in scope, including energy, transport, health, water, digital infrastructure, chemicals, food, waste, and the manufacturing of medical devices, electronics, machinery and vehicles. Suppliers to covered entities are increasingly asked for evidence too.

What are the key dates?

NIS2 entered into force on 16 January 2023. Member states had until 17 October 2024 to turn it into national law and until 17 April 2025 to identify the entities in scope. Obligations apply as each national law takes effect; in Poland, the amended KSC act requires compliance by 3 April 2027.

What does NIS2 require?

Risk management measures (policies, incident handling, business continuity, supply chain security and secure maintenance), incident reporting within 24 hours, 72 hours and one month, and accountability of management bodies, who must approve the measures, take part in training and can be held liable.

What does the readiness assessment deliver?

A gap assessment of each obligation, an inventory of the control network as evidence, an incident playbook for OT and an audit readiness pack, with documents and records organised the way a supervisor will ask for them.

What are the fines?

Up to 10 million euros or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher, for essential entities, and up to 7 million euros or 1.4%, whichever is higher, for important entities.

Is Liscere a law firm?

No. We handle the technical and organisational side and work alongside legal counsel, who should confirm how the national law applies.
