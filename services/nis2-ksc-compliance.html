Deadlines

Four dates every covered company should know.

  • 3 April 2026 In force
    The amended KSC act, Poland's transposition of NIS2, starts to apply. A twelve-month adaptation period begins.
  • 3 October 2026 Passed
    Deadline for entities covered at entry into force to apply for entry in the KSC register (Wykaz KSC). If this applied to you and you have not registered, you are already late. Register now and start the readiness work in parallel.
  • 3 April 2027 Next
    Obligations under the new rules must be implemented, and covered entities must be connected to the S46 system. This is the date to plan around.
  • 3 April 2028 Audit
    First mandatory cybersecurity audit for key entities that were not previously essential service operators. Subsequent audits at least every three years.

Dates as published by the Polish Ministry of Digital Affairs. The act itself governs; your legal counsel should confirm how each date applies to your entity.

Who is covered

Key entities, important entities, and everyone who supplies them.

Key entities (Annex 1)

  • Energy: mining, electricity, heat, oil and fuels, gas, nuclear, hydrogen
  • Transport: air, rail, water, road
  • Banking and financial market infrastructure
  • Health
  • Drinking water supply and distribution
  • Wastewater collection and treatment
  • Digital infrastructure and electronic communications
  • ICT service management, space, public entities

Important entities (Annex 2)

  • Production, manufacturing and distribution of chemicals
  • Production, manufacturing and distribution of food
  • Manufacturing of medical devices, electronics, electrical equipment, machinery, motor vehicles and other transport equipment
  • Waste management
  • Postal services, nuclear investment, digital service providers, research

Coverage depends on enterprise size criteria defined in the act. Not sure where you stand? That is a normal first question, and it is what the scoping call is for. And if you are not covered yourself, your customers may be: NIS2 pushes supply chain security down to suppliers, who are increasingly asked for evidence.

What the act requires

Paperwork is the easy part. Evidence is the hard part.

Covered entities must, among other things:

  • Apply for entry in the KSC register and keep the entry current.
  • Prepare and implement an information security management system (SZBI) that covers the systems delivering the service.
  • Manage risk, protect business continuity and secure the supply chain with measures proportionate to the risk.
  • Detect, handle and report incidents to the competent CSIRT team within the statutory deadlines.
  • Appoint the people responsible for contact with KSC bodies, and verify that relevant personnel have no criminal record.
  • Undergo a mandatory cybersecurity audit (key entities), then repeat it at least every three years.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Does NIS2 apply to my company in Poland?

NIS2 applies in Poland through the amended KSC act. It covers key entities in sectors such as energy, transport, banking, health, drinking water, wastewater and digital infrastructure, and important entities in sectors such as chemicals, food, waste management, postal services and several manufacturing sub-sectors, subject to enterprise size criteria set out in the act. If you are unsure, a short scoping call is usually enough to tell.

What are the NIS2 and KSC deadlines in Poland?

The amended KSC act has applied since April 2026. Entities covered at that point had to apply for entry in the KSC register by 3 October 2026. Obligations under the new rules, and connection to the S46 system, fall due by 3 April 2027. Key entities that were not previously essential service operators must complete a first mandatory cybersecurity audit by 3 April 2028, and then at least every three years.

We missed the 3 October 2026 register deadline. What now?

Register as soon as possible and document why the entry is late. The larger obligations still fall due by 3 April 2027, so the practical priority is to start the readiness work now rather than wait. Liscere can run the gap assessment in parallel with your registration.

Is our OT network in scope?

Yes. The act does not stop at the office network. Risk management, incident handling and audits all cover the systems that deliver the service, which for an industrial company means the control network. Most compliance projects treat it as a black box. Liscere does not.
