Key dates

From directive to national law.

Dates from Directive (EU) 2022/2555. National laws differ in detail; your legal counsel should confirm how they apply to you.

  • In force

    16 January 2023

    NIS2 enters into force at EU level and replaces the original NIS Directive.

  • Transposition

    17 October 2024

    Deadline for member states to turn NIS2 into national law. Many were late, and national laws are still arriving.

  • Registers

    17 April 2025

    Member states to have identified the essential and important entities on their territory.

  • Now

    National deadlines

    Obligations apply as each national law takes effect. In Poland, the amended KSC act requires compliance by 3 April 2027.

Who is covered

Eighteen sectors, and everyone who supplies them.

Medium and large organisations in these sectors are in scope. Not sure where you stand? That is what the scoping call is for.

  • Annex I

    Sectors of high criticality

    • Energy, transport, health
    • Drinking water and wastewater
    • Banking and financial market infrastructure
    • Digital infrastructure, ICT service management, space, public administration
  • Annex II

    Other critical sectors

    • Chemicals and food
    • Manufacturing of medical devices, electronics, electrical equipment, machinery and vehicles
    • Waste management, postal and courier services
    • Digital providers and research
  • Supply chain

    Suppliers to covered entities

    Not covered yourself? Your customers may be. NIS2 pushes security down the supply chain, and suppliers are increasingly asked for evidence.

What NIS2 requires

Paperwork is the easy part. Evidence is the hard part.

An ISMS that does not know what the control network does is paperwork.

  • Risk

    Risk management measures

    Policies, incident handling, business continuity, supply chain security and secure system maintenance, proportionate to the risk.

  • Reporting

    Incident reporting

    An early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours and a final report within one month.

  • Accountability

    Management responsibility

    Management bodies must approve the measures, oversee them and take part in training. They can be held liable.

  • Enforcement

    Supervision and fines

    Up to 10 million euros or 2% of worldwide turnover for essential entities, and 7 million euros or 1.4% for important ones.

Questions we hear

Straight answers.

Does NIS2 apply to my company?

NIS2 covers medium and large organisations in eighteen sectors, from energy, transport, health and water to chemicals, food, manufacturing and waste management. Some smaller entities are covered regardless of size. Each country sets the details in its own law. If you are unsure, a short scoping call is usually enough to tell.

When do the obligations apply?

Member states had to turn NIS2 into national law by 17 October 2024. Many were late, so the obligations take effect country by country as each national law comes into force. In Poland, for example, the amended KSC act requires covered entities to comply by 3 April 2027.

Is our OT network in scope?

Yes. NIS2 does not stop at the office network. Risk management, incident handling and supervision all cover the systems that deliver the service, which for an industrial company means the control network.

Does Liscere replace our legal counsel?

No. We handle the technical and organisational side: the gap assessment, the evidence, the OT visibility, the incident playbook and the audit readiness pack. Questions of legal interpretation stay with your counsel, and we are happy to work alongside them.

How long does a readiness assessment take?

A readiness assessment for a single site typically runs over a few weeks, depending on how much documentation already exists and whether OT visibility is included. Scope and price are fixed up front after a scoping call.
